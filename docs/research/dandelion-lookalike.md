# Dandelion lookalike research: *Hypochaeris radicata* (Task A)

Research input for the Dandelion (*Taraxacum officinale*) Boss Form pilot (`docs/boss-form-implementation-plan.md`).
Fetched 2026-11-12. Every quotation below was copied from text returned by the cited URL in this session.
Nothing here is a botanical determination; it is a record of what pages said.

## 1. Bottom line

- **Is *H. radicata* a well-supported lookalike of dandelion? Yes, but the evidence base I could actually read is thinner than the brief asked for.** Penn State Extension (read directly) and the English Wikipedia articles *Taraxacum* and *Hypochaeris radicata* (read directly; the *Taraxacum* article cites a printed flora, Stace, that I could **not** read) all say it is commonly mistaken for dandelion and give the same two main differences: forked vs unforked flowering stems, and hairy vs hairless leaves.
- **Not achieved, stated plainly:** I could not read **USDA PLANTS** or **Flora of North America (efloras.org)**. Details in section 6. So the brief's "at least two credible sources per character" is met only where Penn State and Wikipedia-citing-Stace both speak, and Wikipedia's *H. radicata* article is **not independent** of Penn State (section 4, item 3).
- **Hollow stalks: the plan's note is not what the sources say.** The plan (section E.2 of `boss-form-implementation-plan.md`) says "Sources say both plants have hollow stalks". The sources I read say the opposite for cat's-ear: **dandelion hollow, catsear solid.** See section 3.4, including why a question should still not use hollowness.

## 3. Diagnostic characters

"Dandelion" means *Taraxacum officinale* as described by the source. Quotes are verbatim.

### 3.1 Flower-stalk branching

| Source | Exact quoted sentence |
|---|---|
| PSU-C | "Catsear flowering stems are forked and solid with multiple flowers per plant, whereas dandelions possess unforked stems that are hollow with a single flower." |
| WP-T (cites Stace, *New Flora of the British Isles*, p. 740, **not read by me**) | "However, dandelion have unbranched, hairless and leafless, hollow stems, while cat's ear stems are branched, solid, and carry bracts." |
| WP-HR | "Forked stems carry bright yellow flower heads" (Botany section); and "However, catsear flowering stems are forked and solid, whereas dandelions possess unforked, hollow stems (peduncles)." |

**Agreement:** all say catsear's flowering stem branches/forks; dandelion's does not.
**Caution:** PSU-C's "single flower" per dandelion plant is loose. A dandelion plant normally has several flowering stems, each with one head; WP-TO says "several hollow, leafless flower stems" and "each flowering stem lacks bracts and has one single flower head". Questions should say **one head per stalk**, not "one flower per plant".

### 3.2 Leaf hairiness and shape

| Source | Exact quoted sentence |
|---|---|
| PSU-C | "The leaves of catsear are dark green, lobe-shaped and hairy which is in stark contrast to dandelions, which have jagged, hairless leaves that are a lighter shade of green." |
| WP-T | "However, the leaves of dandelions are smooth, whereas those of cat's ear are hairy." |
| WP-HR (Botany) | "The leaves, which may grow up to eight inches (20 cm) long, are lobed and covered in coarse hairs, forming a low-lying rosette around a central taproot." |

**Agreement:** hairy (catsear) vs hairless/smooth (dandelion).
**Caution:** PSU-C's colour contrast ("dark green" vs "lighter shade of green") is a single-source claim that I did not see repeated; do not use leaf colour in a question. PSU-C's "jagged" for dandelion is consistent with PSU-D ("Mature foliage is deeply toothed, with the teeth pointing back towards the base of the plant"), but "lobe-shaped" for catsear is vague; shape is a weaker character than hairiness.

### 3.3 Scale-like bracts on the flower stalk

| Source | Exact quoted sentence |
|---|---|
| WP-T | "...cat's ear stems are branched, solid, and carry bracts." |
| WP-TO | "...each flowering stem lacks bracts and has one single flower head." |
| PSU-C, PSU-D | **Nothing.** Neither page says anything about bracts on the stalk. |

**Status: single-source (Wikipedia, citing a print flora I could not read).** The plan's wording "small scale-like bracts" is not a phrase I found in any source; the sources say "bracts" only. **Do not write a question on this character until a second source is read.** The reference photographs (see `dandelion-images.md`) are not evidence for it.

### 3.4 Stalk hollowness

| Source | Exact quoted sentence |
|---|---|
| PSU-C | "...dandelions possess unforked stems that are hollow with a single flower." and "Catsear flowering stems are forked and solid..." |
| WP-T | "...dandelion have unbranched, hairless and leafless, hollow stems, while cat's ear stems are branched, solid..." |
| WP-HR | "However, catsear flowering stems are forked and solid, whereas dandelions possess unforked, hollow stems (peduncles)." |
| WP-TO | "...produces several hollow, leafless flower stems..." (cites Rose, *The Wild Flower Key*, p. 470; **not read by me**) |
| PSU-D | No statement about hollowness. |

**What the sources really say:** dandelion hollow, catsear **solid**. I found **no** source saying both are hollow. The plan's "both hollow" statement is therefore unsupported by what I read; it may come from a source I could not access (USDA / FNA) or from search snippets. I cannot resolve that, so it is a **conflict between the plan and the sources, unresolved.**

**Even so, do not use hollowness as a question's answer.** Reasons, all my own judgement and not citations: (a) the Penn State and Wikipedia-HR wording is the same sentence, so the "solid" claim is effectively one source repeated; (b) hollow vs solid cannot be checked from a photograph; (c) cutting open stems of an unidentified plant is a field action this product should not prompt; (d) the plan already ruled hollowness out as a discriminator. Branching and leaf hairiness are the supported ones.

### 3.5 Flower head size

**No source I read gives a flower-head size for either plant.** PSU-C, PSU-D, WP-T, WP-TO and WP-HR contain no comparative head diameter. Not supported; do not use.

## 4. Where sources disagree, or the plan disagrees with them

1. **Hollow stalks (plan vs sources).** Plan: both hollow. Sources read: dandelion hollow, catsear solid. See 3.4.
2. **"Single flower" per dandelion plant (PSU-C) vs "several hollow, leafless flower stems" with "one single flower head" per stem (WP-TO).** A wording issue, not a botanical conflict; use "one head per stalk".
3. **Independence.** WP-HR's passage on forked/solid stems and lobed/hairy leaves is near-verbatim identical to PSU-C ("catsear flowering stems are forked and solid, whereas dandelions possess unforked, hollow stems" appears in both). I believe one was derived from the other; I cannot prove direction. **Count PSU-C and WP-HR as one source for branching, hairiness and solidity.** The only apparently separate corroboration is WP-T, which cites Stace (p. 740), a printed flora I have not seen.
4. **Dates** are as displayed on the pages on 2026-11-12; I did not verify them independently.

## 5. *Taraxacum officinale* as a species complex

Only Wikipedia text was readable. Quotes:

- "The genus is taxonomically complex due to the presence of apomixis; any morphologically distinct clonal population would deserve its own microspecies." (WP-T)
- "The taxonomy of this genus has been complicated by the recognition of numerous species, subspecies and microspecies." (WP-TO)
- "...the taxonomy and nomenclatural situation of T. officinale is not yet fully resolved." (WP-TO; its wikitext attributes this to an eFloras citation I could not open, so I cannot confirm that eFloras says it.)
- "The plants introduced to North America, originally as a food crop, are triploids that mainly reproduce by obligate gametophytic apomixis" (WP-TO).
- Penn State (PSU-D): "Seeds can be produced without pollination." It does not use the word "complex" or "aggregate".

**The string "T. officinale agg." appeared in no page I read.** The only "complex" wording is the *title* of a CABI datasheet cited in WP-TO's wikitext ("Taraxacum officinale complex (dandelion)"); I saw that citation title only, not the datasheet, and cabi.org is outside the allowed hosts. I cannot say what CABI states.

**Implication:** no question should make a species-level claim inside *Taraxacum* (the plan already says so).

## 7. Other lookalikes (no recommendation beyond one)

**Recommended single lookalike: *Hypochaeris radicata*.** Others I saw named, each from one source only:

- *Crepis* (hawksbeard) and *Hieracium* (hawkweed): WP-T says "Other plants with superficially similar flowers include hawkweeds (Hieracium) and hawksbeards (Crepis). These are distinguished by branched stems, which are usually hairy and bear leaves." (cites Stace p. 740, not read).
- *Tussilago farfara* (coltsfoot), early spring: WP-T, citing Blamey et al. (not read).
- *Scorzoneroides autumnalis* (autumn hawkbit) and *Hypochaeris glabra*: WP-HR, as lookalikes **of catsear**, not of dandelion.
