## 3. ShareAlike obligations (plain statement, not legal advice)

The three ShareAlike files are `dandelion-flower-stalk` (CC BY-SA 3.0), `hypochaeris-branched-stalks` (CC BY-SA 4.0) and `hypochaeris-leaf-hairs` (CC BY-SA 3.0). ## 5. Method notes

- Commons returned HTTP 429 to several requests, including `upload.wikimedia.org` image fetches, so some candidates were not evaluated; I retried with backoff and did not change identity or route.
- 640px is not an allowed Wikimedia thumbnail step (the host answered 400 "Use thumbnail sizes listed"); I fetched 960px and resized locally. This means the "original" was never in my sandbox, only Wikimedia's derivative, so any compression or sharpening in that rendition is inherited.
- To fit the limit, two accepted files were reduced below 640px (flower head 560px at quality 58; flower stalk 440px at quality 67); the rejected rosette would have needed 480px at quality 55. That cost resolution in exactly the files where fine detail matters; see the rosette rejection above.
- Penn State Extension, Wikipedia (both read for Task A) are cited in `dandelion-lookalike.md`. These photographs are illustrations, **not** evidence for any character; the characters stand or fall on the quoted text.

## 6. Unresolved concerns

1. **No botanist has reviewed any file.** Every identification is the uploader's. In particular I cannot tell *H. radicata* from other cat's-ears, hawkbits or hawkweeds, and Commons contains at least one misfiled example (rejected above).
2. **Slot 6 is unmet as specified.** There is no *H. radicata* **rosette** in the set. The supplied leaf close-up shows hairs but is not a rosette and is not a comparison pair with a dandelion leaf. Decide whether a question can rest on a single-leaf image, or whether more candidates should be searched, and with a larger per-file budget than 45 KB.
3. **The 45 KB / 640px budget hurts the fine-detail slots.** Hair, bract and floret detail is what the questions need and is what compression removed first. Consider raising the budget for these specific images, or using crops (with a changes note) rather than whole frames.
4. **A dandelion photo showing a hairy, bract-like stalk exists** (`stem4`, rejected). If real dandelion stalks can carry cobwebby hairs near the head, then "hairless" is true of the leaves and not safely true of the whole plant, and the sources' flat "hairless" for the stem may be too strong. I cannot resolve this; a reviewer should, before any question says the stalk is hairless.
5. **Solid vs hollow stalks** is in tension with the plan; see `dandelion-lookalike.md` section 3.4. No image here shows it, and none should be captioned as if it did.
6. **Scale-like bracts on catsear stalks** are single-sourced in Task A and not demonstrated in any accepted photograph; do not caption any image as showing them.
7. **"Both a rosette" photo for dandelion (slot 1)** is a single leaf. If the question is "which is the rosette leaf", the image is adequate; if it is "recognise a rosette", it is not.
8. **Dates on some pages are in 2026** (for example the 12 June 2026 upload date of the two TomStonehunter files, and the Penn State "Updated: August 14, 2026"). I recorded them as displayed and did not question them.
9. **Wikipedia versus the primary floras.** FNA and USDA PLANTS could not be read (see `dandelion-lookalike.md` section 6), so none of the characters these photographs illustrate rests on a primary flora in this work.
10. **ShareAlike scope** is stated in section 3 as my reading; a human should confirm the obligations on each copy before shipping.
11. **Attribution display.** The attribution strings in the JSON are drafted from the file-page fields. Commons also generates its own "Use this file" credit text in the browser, which I could not fetch; compare against it before publishing.
