## (c) Safety caution

Question: What can the 'cyanide' caution honestly say about white clover?

- **Some individuals release HCN from damaged leaves** ([hughes1991], [kakes1994], [olsen2008]): Cyanogenesis is a polymorphism: cyanogenic and acyanogenic plants coexist; HCN requires tissue damage; restricted to leaf blades; content varies widely among plants.
- **Quantities generally modest but variable** ([muzashvili2014], [stochmal1994]): Survey: generally below 500 micrograms/g dry matter, with extreme outliers in a variety; one cultivar had about 4.4 mg/g combined. Samples of one species ranged from none to high.
- **Livestock bloat** ([purdue-clover]): White clover is a bloat-causing legume for cattle, sheep and goats (not cyanide).

**Where sources disagree / what is unresolved:** No source disagrees; the human relevance is a gap. NO source read states how much HCN, if any, matters for people eating leaves or flowers (NOT FOUND). The draft's 'Some plants can release cyanide' is accurate for T. repens but unspecific.

**Draft card warning:** "Some plants can release cyanide." - SUPPORTED in substance (some white clover plants are cyanogenic) but written generically ('some plants'), which hides that it is THIS plant and says nothing about the part (leaf blades) or variability. Not preparation advice. Better to be specific, not stronger.

**Draft guide caution:** None in the draft (null).

**Proposed site caution:** PROPOSED DRAFT FOR REVIEW, NOT FINAL WORDING: 'Some white clover plants release hydrogen cyanide from their leaves when the tissue is damaged; others do not, and you cannot tell which by looking. How much matters for people has not been established in the sources reviewed. Plantdex does not recommend eating wild plants.'

**What the botanist must check against the sources**

- Leaf: three leaflets, toothed margin, pale crescent/V (drawn and visible; sources say it is often present, not always). Confirm toothing is fine and regular (UMD) and the leaflets are unstalked (MDC, UMD).
- Flower-stalk surface: the two stalks in the image carry a conspicuous scaly/hatched, hairy-looking texture. Sources describe stems and leaflets as smooth or minimally hairy (UMD, UC IPM, Purdue 'no pubescence'; MDC allows 'hairy or not'). Possible generation artefact.
- Flower heads: confirm florets are white to pinkish (the image shows strong pink tinting), floret count and form (MDC 20-50; NRCS 40-100), and the calyx: unequally toothed, often with dark purple at the V indentations (MDC). The image shows long green-and-reddish spiky calyx tips; verify they are not an artefact.
- Bud: a conical pink-red bud at the stem junction looks more like a red-clover bud than a white clover one; confirm.
- Stalk attachment: heads should arise singly on unbranched leafless stalks from the creeping stem at a leaf node, with no pair of leaflike bracts (MDC). The image shows two stalks joining a thick stem at a single point with the leaf; check that this matches the real arrangement.
- Habit: stolon rooting at nodes (the field mark that separates it from alsike) is not shown; the leaf stalk is short relative to the 8 in length MDC reports. Check the card does not imply otherwise.
- Scale/proportions: leaflets are 0.6-1.2 cm (UC IPM) against heads of 1/2-1 1/2 in (MDC); check relative sizes are plausible.
- Cut-out edges: a pink/white halo is visible along the flower and leaf edges, typical of an automatic cut-out.
- Colour: leaflet green with a pale chevron looks plausible; check against herbarium or reliable photographs.
